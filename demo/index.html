How to read this

Each session starts with three games the player already saw. The ranker uses those as a hint. The rest of the games are the slate, and both rankers put the slate in order. Only the top five count toward the score.

The last column shows what the player did with each game, with the grade the score uses: 0 skipped or quit, 1 played for 20 seconds or more, 2 finished, 3 replayed or shared. A good order puts finished and replayed games near the top.

Under each game in our ranking, a plus sign marks a reason that pushed it up and a minus sign marks one that pulled it down.

Most of the gain comes from the player's own record with a game's family and mechanic. Taste decides what gets started, and fit decides what gets finished.

Players with two or fewer earlier sessions gain much less.

The 40 sessions here are a picked sample.

About the data

This is an offline replay. The slates are the games an earlier feed chose to show, and the outcomes are what players did with them in that feed. A ranker that would have shown different games cannot be judged here. The numbers measure how well each ranker orders games that were already on screen.

Reason labels come from the biggest pushes and pulls on each game's score, grouped and put in plain words. They describe what moved the ranker's score and say nothing about the player's motives.

There is no game art, so each game is described by its family, mechanic and difficulty.
